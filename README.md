# CS 250 Portfolio — Software Development Lifecycle

This repository contains my Sprint Review and Retrospective for the SNHU Travel project in CS 250. Throughout the course, I practiced the Scrum Master, Product Owner, developer, and tester roles in a course simulation. The project helped me connect user needs, user stories, development, and testing while responding to a change in direction toward wellness travel.

## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I start by understanding what users want to accomplish and asking questions when a requirement is unclear. User stories help me break those needs into smaller pieces by identifying the user, the feature they need, and why it matters. For SNHU Travel, separating destination browsing from preferences and budget controls made the work easier to prioritize. Adding acceptance criteria gave me specific behavior to build and test, such as whether a package at the user's maximum price should appear. I also learned that a working slideshow does not mean every related user story is complete; each feature still needs to meet its own requirements.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach development by breaking a larger problem into manageable tasks, building a small part, and checking that it works before adding more. In future projects, I want to use a prioritized backlog, short development cycles, and regular feedback to keep the work focused on user needs. The change to wellness travel showed me the value of adapting the plan and reusing useful work. I also want to use retrospectives to choose specific improvements, such as resolving unclear acceptance criteria before starting a story, and then check whether those changes helped during the next sprint.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member means communicating clearly, following through on commitments, and raising questions or blockers early. It also means listening to feedback and helping the team reach its goal. Developers, testers, and the Product Owner need a shared understanding of what a feature should do so they do not make conflicting assumptions. I would contribute by keeping my progress visible, documenting decisions, being honest about unfinished work, and discussing problems respectfully so the team can find a solution together.
